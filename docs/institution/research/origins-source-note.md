## 1. Source classification

Three documents were ingested:

1. `laltro_spazio_origini_concise_bilingual_no_senza_nome.docx` — a short, poetic, heavily compressed bilingual (IT/EN) prologue, organised as: Nunzia (gaze) → Jascha (translation) → NDSM/Bologna → "Le Mani" (hands, Parco, water-damage rupture) → Via Nazario (construction).
2. `laltro_spazio_traditional_bilingual_origins_no_senza_nome.docx` — the fullest, most sequential version, organised in six numbered sections (Origini 2010 → La Domanda → Jascha → Bologna → Via Nazario → Preparativi e Apertura), written substantially in Nunzia's and Jascha's first person within their respective sections.
3. `origini_nunzia_jascha_noi_bilingual.pdf` — a "pure text" bilingual version structurally organised into three clearly separated first-person voices: **Nunzia**, **Jascha**, **Noi/We**, ending at the same "it is a life" close as the other two.

**Classification: OWNER / ORAL-HISTORY NARRATIVE DRAFT (retrospective editorial narrative).** All three are edited literary treatments of the same underlying owner memory and prior interview material, prepared for eventual public/institutional storytelling use. They are **not three independent sources** — they are one narrative expressed in three overlapping editorial forms, and **repetition of a detail across all three documents is not corroboration**; it is the same memory copy-edited three times. Where this note cites a detail, it treats it as coming from **one** narrative source, occasionally noting which version phrases it most fully.

**Strong for:** owner perspective, lived experience, motivation, remembered process, design intent, language/voice, phenomenological detail, the relationship between art, communication, and space, and how the founders understood what they were doing.

**Not independently authoritative for:** exact dates, legal entities, ownership, company formation, formal programme status, financial facts, or objective causal claims. Where any date/entity detail here conflicts with the stronger formal or Facebook-archive evidence already in `facebook/facebook-historical-evidence-register.md` and `facebook/institutional-timeline-v0.3-draft.md`, **that stronger evidence wins** — see §4.

## 3. Overlapping provenance — explicit non-corroboration warning

All three documents describe, with only stylistic variation:
- Nunzia's 2010 encounter with the Deaf community at Parco 11 Settembre and the customer's "normal people" remark during "a first project" (unnamed in all three — see §4).
- Jascha's Rietveld/interpreter experience, his NDSM isolation, and Nunzia's invitation to Bologna after seeing "I Sign, I Live" in Rome.
- The water-damage closure of "the bar Nunzia had recently opened" as the rupture that led the two of them to imagine a new joint project.
- The Via Nazario Sauro office, the SRL formation/notary episode, and the construction period (tape, moodboards, first team, opening night).

**This is one memory, recorded three times for three different editorial purposes.** Nothing in this note treats the number "three" as adding evidentiary weight beyond what a single well-corroborated owner account already carries.

## 4. Critical correction preserved: Via Polese is not erased by this narrative

**None of the three documents names Via Polese, Associazione L'Altro Spazio, or "Senza Nome" explicitly** — this is deliberate (see the traditional version's own subtitle: "revised without naming the earlier bar project," and the concise version's filename, "no_senza_nome"). Instead, all three refer to an unnamed "first project" / "first experiment" / "the bar Nunzia had recently opened," which — per the prior correction pass (`facebook/facebook-historical-evidence-register.md` C-FB-01) — **is Via Polese 7 (2014)**, run through Associazione L'Altro Spazio. The narrative's own internal details (a bar built around sign language where Deaf staff are professionals rather than assisted customers; closing after water damage; leading directly to a decision to build something new together) match the already-established Via Polese chronology precisely — this narrative is describing Via Polese, just without naming it.

**The specific wording to flag:** the traditional version states, in Nunzia's section: *"Da quelle conversazioni nacque L'Altro Spazio"* / *"From those conversations, L'Altro Spazio was born."* Read in isolation, this could mislead a future reader into thinking the L'Altro Spazio name/project originated with these post-Polese conversations. **It must not be read that way.** The name and the underlying idea (a hospitality space built around Deaf presence and sign language) already existed at Via Polese in 2014, per the account's own Facebook posts and the owner's confirmed history. The correct reading, preserved here explicitly:

> "From those conversations, **the new Via Nazario incarnation** / the new shared L'Altro Spazio project **emerged**" — not the first historical origin of the name or the underlying idea.

This is consistent with the narrative's own next sentence in the same passage ("When it opened in Via Nazario Sauro, in 2015…"), which ties the "birth" language to the 2015 opening specifically, not to an undated absolute beginning. **Status: CONFLICT NOTED AND RESOLVED** — the editorial phrase is retained as an interesting piece of the narrative's own voice, but it is not used as a chronological claim, and this note exists specifically so no future draft repeats it uncritically.

**Second correction preserved:** none of the three documents should be read as implying one uninterrupted legal entity across 2010/2014 onward. The practice/brand lineage (Nunzia's 2010 Parco encounter → Via Polese/Associazione L'Altro Spazio 2014 → Via Nazario/new 50/50 L'Altro Spazio SRL 2015 →, separately, Associazione Farm's own Parco strand →, later, L'Altro Spazio Pratello SRLS 2018 →, later, Cooperativa L'Altro Spazio 2022) remains a **practice/brand continuity**, not a single legal entity's continuous history. Nothing in these narrative documents changes that — if anything, the documents' own careful avoidance of naming any specific entity supports keeping the practice and legal-entity tracks separate.

**Third correction preserved:** narrative timing does not override formal/archive dates. Where the narrative is vague or scene-setting (e.g. the notary visit, the opening night), the Facebook archive's real-time posts (C-FB-03: opening night posts 26-29 Oct 2015) remain the stronger chronological anchor. See §12 for one specific instance (opening-night timing) where this matters.

## 5. The GAZE + TRANSLATION → SPACE/STRUCTURE → PARTICIPATION hypothesis (status: INTERPRETATION — not yet a conclusion)

The clearest new analytical opportunity these documents offer is a candidate intellectual genealogy for the seven working propositions, distinguishing how each founder first encountered exclusion:

- **Nunzia's route into the practice is through GAZE / VISIBILITY / SOCIAL IMAGINATION**: she does not initially experience exclusion herself — she witnesses it, then is struck by a customer's assumption ("I never imagined Deaf people were normal people") that reveals how *invisibility* in social imagination, not impairment, produces exclusion. Her own formulation: *"Non mancavano loro. Erano i nostri spazi a essere stati costruiti senza pensarle"* ("They were not missing. Our spaces had simply been built without them in mind.")
- **Jascha's route into the practice is through TRANSLATION / MEDIATION / POSITION WITHIN INTERACTION**: his exclusion is experienced directly and structurally, as a lifelong condition of being routed through interpreters — "close to the conversation, but never completely inside it" — and of living between two worlds (Deaf friends, art world) without being fully inside either.
- **The shared project then moves the question from a personal experience of exclusion toward SPACE / STRUCTURE / PARTICIPATION**: Via Nazario is imagined and built, quite literally with tape and floor plans, as an answer to both routes into the problem at once — a space where gaze is redirected (Deaf/disabled staff visible as professionals, not curiosities) and where translation is designed-in rather than added afterward (sign language as an ordinary transactional mode, per the archive's own later "Karaoke Ignorante" copy, C-FB in the Facebook register).

**Proposed progression:** GAZE + TRANSLATION → SPACE/STRUCTURE → PARTICIPATION.

This is offered as a **high-value interpretive hypothesis**, not a settled fact. It is attractive because it would let Working Paper v0.3 present the seven propositions as **emerging from two people's distinct, lived routes into the same problem**, rather than as abstractions invented after the fact and then illustrated with anecdotes. But it rests on retrospective narrative material written for storytelling purposes, and should be tested against the interview corpus (S01-S12 in Working Paper v0.2) and, where possible, against material from a period closer to the events themselves before being treated as established intellectual history.

## 6. Nunzia's perspective (kept distinct — not merged with Jascha's)

- 2010: while working on an urban-regeneration project at Parco 11 Settembre, she notices a group of Deaf people signing, is struck by it ("it looked like a dance"), and begins spending time with the Deaf community — self-taught, informal, "in love with the language before I knew its rules." **[C-ORIG-01]**
- Through those friendships she comes to see "invisible barriers" separating people from social/cultural/professional life, leading to "a first project" (unnamed — Via Polese) built around sign language, with Deaf staff as professionals rather than assisted customers. **[C-ORIG-02]**
- The customer's "normal people" remark is placed explicitly during the first months of *that* project (i.e. at Via Polese, not Via Nazario) — an important placement, since it means the archive's 2014 deafblind/Deaf-staffed venue was already demonstrating the "staff as professionals, not curiosities" model the org is known for later. **[C-ORIG-03]**
- From that moment she frames the problem as "society's imagination," not disability — generalising immediately to blind people, wheelchair users, and people with intellectual disabilities as similarly "not missing" but un-imagined. **[C-ORIG-04]**
- She invites Jascha to Bologna after seeing "I Sign, I Live" at a Rome film festival, initially as "an artistic invitation" — she is explicit that "only later did we understand that this encounter would open much more." **[C-ORIG-05]**

Status for all of the above: **ATTRIBUTED / OWNER NARRATIVE.** C-ORIG-01 and C-ORIG-03 are **consistent with, and add first-months precision to,** the already-established C-FB-01 (Via Polese) in the Facebook register — this narrative describes the same venue without naming it (see §4).

## 7. Jascha's perspective (kept distinct — not merged with Nunzia's)

- Rietveld Academy (Amsterdam): a "difficult period," never quite receiving confirmation, always behind conversations mediated through interpreters — "close to the conversation, but never completely inside it." Living with "two worlds" (Deaf friends; the art world) and belonging fully to neither, "always translating between different worlds, without being completely inside one." **[C-ORIG-06]**
- Post-graduation: a confused, at times depressed period; a workspace at Kunststad/NDSM (Amsterdam), working alone in a shared container, on "the tension between real and unreal, virtual and physical" — inviting Deaf friends there partly just "not to be alone." **[C-ORIG-07]**
- Invited to Bologna by Nunzia to present "I Sign, I Live," do a VJ set, and show work — accepts, flies to Bologna, meets her; describes being "overwhelmed" by her curiosity/energy/intuition toward sign language, and by Italy generally. A later reunion in Venice, "just the two of us," is when personal feelings begin. **[C-ORIG-08]**
- That summer, working behind the bar at the Parco 11 Settembre festival for the first time, he is "astonished" that communication is easier than expected — customers took time, used gesture, "were not afraid of hands" — giving him "a strange comfort" and prompting an explicit comparison ("in the Netherlands it probably would not be like this"). **[C-ORIG-09]** *(This directly corroborates, from the inside, the archive-independent 2021 Redattore Sociale "acqua in LIS" observation and the general Parco-as-site-of-normalised-gesture pattern already documented in `facebook/programme-history.md` §4.)*
- On the water-damage closure: "For me it was a huge step. Hospitality was an unknown world, but I felt it could become something important." **[C-ORIG-10]**

Status: **ATTRIBUTED / OWNER NARRATIVE.** C-ORIG-06 is the single strongest new piece of narrative evidence for Proposition 2 found in any source reviewed so far (see §13).

## 8. The shared "Noi/We" perspective (kept distinct — not collapsed into an institutional voice)

- The water-damage closure ("it felt like all the problems arrived at once") is the explicit joint pivot point: *"Fu allora che cominciammo a immaginare qualcosa da fare insieme"* ("That was when we began to imagine something we could do together") — "something new, with a special focus on art and disability… not disability as a theme. Disability inside the structure." **[C-ORIG-11]**
- Finding the Via Nazario office: the landlord's enthusiasm on learning Nunzia was a teacher wanting to involve "education, learning, and social life"; the founders themselves speaking of "activity, encounter, a social space," not yet "only about a bar." **[C-ORIG-12]** — see §10 (design/construction evidence).
- The notary/SRL episode (§11).
- Construction: tape on floor and walls, floor plans, moodboards, "mostly ideas and dust," then pipes/electricity/water/wood/people; the first team (Manuela, Michele, Chiara, Serena, "and others"); painting the whole venue themselves; opening despite unfinished work, guests already waiting outside. **[C-ORIG-13]** — see §10, §12.
- Closing formulation, present in all three versions with only minor variation: *"Disability was no longer an idea. It was a daily structure. It is a life."*

Status: **ATTRIBUTED / OWNER NARRATIVE**, jointly authored/reviewed by both founders per the document's own structure.

## 9. Cross-reference: the "first team" names also appear independently elsewhere

**Manuela, Michele, and Chiara** are not only named in this origin narrative — they are also the named interview subjects **S05 (Manuela), S02 (Michele), and S04 (Chiara)** in Working Paper v0.2 Appendix A, and **Serena** matches **Serena Sacco**, credited in the Facebook archive as "L'Altro Spazio's own cook" in the 2017 Chef Rubio "ricette accessibili" collaboration (`facebook/programme-history.md` §5-6, from the 2016-2017 Facebook chunk analysis). This is a genuine, useful convergence across three different evidence types — origin narrative, interview corpus, and Facebook archive — naming the same small group of people as central to the venue's early life. **This is still not "triple corroboration" of any single fact** (each source documents a different thing: participation in construction, testimony about work/life at the venue, and a specific 2017 media collaboration respectively) — but it is a coherent, mutually-reinforcing picture of who the first team was, and is worth using as such.

**Preserve uncertainty, per instruction:** participating in the build-out and opening period is not automatically equivalent to being formally employed from day one. None of the three source types (narrative, interview, archive) establishes exact employment start dates or contract status for any of these individuals, and this note does not infer any.

## 10. Design/construction as evidence

**New evidence cluster: "Via Nazario was consciously designed as a social/cultural environment, not merely fitted out as a conventional bar."**

Supporting detail from the owner narrative (status: **OWNER-NARRATIVE / DESIGN-INTENT EVIDENCE** throughout — not independently verified against any surviving physical/photographic record in this pass):

- The founders spoke to the landlord, and to themselves, in terms of "activity, encounter, a social space" before "bar" language — and Jascha recalls this "felt like the right language, because it was true."
- A conscious "gallery with a bar" concept, "still very influenced by Rietveld" — a large white space where the bar is "only one part of the activity," with exhibitions intended to change over time.
- Deliberate material choices: birch/light wood for bar structure and practical elements, to "give stronger presence to the artworks"; white "because it felt clear, with less noise."
- Two specific, named visual works: a 30-sheet A4 poster forming a SpaceX rocket-test image, and a hand-painted "galaxy map" mural inspired by "a signal, a deviation in frequency" — which Jascha links explicitly and personally to searching for signals "perhaps because sound never came easily to me" (see §13, Proposition 6).
- Spatial prototyping method: tape on floor and walls to imagine where things could happen, hand-drawn floor plans and moodboards, reference-gathering (visiting other bars, flea markets, vintage shops) before construction began.
- A first build team combining trade specialists (an architect, a fast/skilled woodworker, a plumber-electrician) with the future venue workers themselves, "many of them disabled" — i.e. the people who would later work front-of-house were present during, and participating in, the physical construction.

**Caution, per instruction:** this evidence supports *design intent* and a *general participatory building process* — it should not be overstated into a claim that every specific accessibility feature (Braille menus, lowered counter, sign-language ordering — independently documented via the 2018 Lonely Planet feature, `facebook/facebook-archive-chronology.md` 2018) was itself co-designed with the same deliberateness as the aesthetic programme described here. The narrative is much more detailed about the *art/aesthetic* design intent than about the *accessibility-feature* design process specifically.

## 11. The notary/translation episode

During formation of the new 50/50 L'Altro Spazio SRL, the deed reportedly required official English translation because Jascha — Deaf, Dutch, and unable to read Italian — needed to understand what he was signing; Nunzia translated/interpreted for him, and the deed itself reportedly records that he was able to read English and therefore understood the document. Nunzia recalls church bells ringing at the exact moment she signed, and having "goosebumps"; the moment was filmed, and the two went for a celebratory drink afterward. Jascha's own words: "It felt almost like a marriage."

**Status: OWNER-CONFIRMED (the translation accommodation itself, at a general level) / ATTRIBUTED-OWNER-MEMORY (the bell-ringing, goosebumps, and "almost a marriage" framing specifically) / DOCUMENTARY FOLLOW-UP POSSIBLE.** This narrative draft is **not** treated as documentary proof of the actual deed's contents or exact wording.

**This episode is genuinely interesting beyond colour**: it places translation/accessibility not only in the organisation's later public-facing practice, but in the very **act of the organisation's own legal formation** — a nice, concrete instance of Proposition 2 operating at the level of the company's founding paperwork, not just its hospitality service.

**New research question (non-blocking):** can the original 2015 incorporation/notarial deed be located and reviewed to corroborate the translation procedure and its specific wording? This is a useful future archival target, not a blocker for current work.

## 12. Where narrative timing should defer to the archive

The traditional version recalls the opening happening "an hour after planned," with guests already waiting outside while construction was still being finished. The Facebook archive's real-time posts (`facebook/facebook-historical-evidence-register.md` C-FB-03) show the account posting "Stiamo aprendo! Alé!" at 19:14 on 29 October 2015 — only ~14 minutes after the announced 19:00 start, not a full hour. **The archive's real-time post timestamp is treated as the stronger record of actual opening timing; the "an hour late" detail is retained as a plausible, vivid, but imprecise memory of how chaotic the final push felt, not as a correction to the established opening time.** This is exactly the kind of case the source-classification rule in §1 anticipates.

## 13. Seven propositions — updated evidence map

For each proposition: **NARRATIVE** (this pass) / **ARCHIVE** (Facebook, prior passes) / **INTERVIEW** (Working Paper v0.2 interview corpus, S01-S12) / **FORMAL** (primary/formal records) / **INTERPRETATION** (analytical synthesis). A single narrative source is counted once regardless of how many of the three documents phrase it.

**Proposition 1** — people are limited by systems built from hidden assumptions, not by themselves.
- NARRATIVE: Nunzia's shift from "the problem was deafness" to "the problem was society's imagination… our spaces had simply been built without them in mind" (C-ORIG-04). Strong new support — arguably the cleanest single statement of this proposition found in any source.
- ARCHIVE: the 2023 priority-booking/wheelchair-routing policy (C-FB-19); the Redattore Sociale "acqua in Lis" observation.
- INTERVIEW: Maurice (S01) on handicap as relational.
- INTERPRETATION: Working Paper v0.2 §6, Proposition 1 commentary — now has a clearer origin story to cite.

**Proposition 2** — translation is not added afterward; it is how meaning is created.
- NARRATIVE: Jascha's Rietveld/interpreter account — "close to the conversation, but never completely inside it" (C-ORIG-06). The single strongest new narrative support of any proposition in this pass.
- ARCHIVE: the 2023 university-LIS-professor/Ente Nazionale Sordi event (C-FB-18); the 2025 Sub_Bar Show/Sensory Dialogues (EU-funded).
- INTERVIEW: Daniel (S03); Jascha's founding narrative (S11), now enriched with the specific Rietveld/interpreter detail this narrative source adds.
- FORMAL: the notary/translation episode (§11) — a unique instance of this proposition operating in the organisation's own legal formation, not just its public programme.

**Proposition 3** — a space, process, or design communicates before a word is spoken.
- NARRATIVE: the Via Nazario design-intent cluster (§10) — spatial prototyping, "activity/encounter/social space" language preceding "bar" language, gallery-with-bar concept. The richest new evidence for any single proposition in this pass, alongside Proposition 2.
- ARCHIVE: Lonely Planet's independent description of physical accessibility features (Braille menus, lowered counter); the 2023 wheelchair-routing directions.
- INTERPRETATION: directly feeds the "practice of environments" hypothesis already flagged as INTERPRETATION in `facebook/facebook-historical-evidence-register.md` C-FB-27 — this narrative material strengthens, without proving, that hypothesis.

**Proposition 4** — good design reduces the compensatory effort required to participate.
- NARRATIVE: the Parco gesture/communication-ease passage ("people took time… were not afraid of hands… that gave me a strange comfort," C-ORIG-09); the deliberate reduction of "visual noise" in the venue's aesthetic design (§10).
- ARCHIVE: the 2023 explicit priority-booking policy (C-FB-19).
- INTERVIEW: Chiara's beer-tap adaptation (S04); Manuela's counter-design account (S05).

**Proposition 5** — identity emerges through participation, not representation alone.
- NARRATIVE: the first team physically building the venue (§9) — the people who would work there were present in its construction, not simply hired into a finished space.
- ARCHIVE: the 2018 hiring-policy statement; the 2022 (unverified) 80% figure (C-FB-20).
- INTERVIEW: the same individuals — Manuela (S05), Michele (S02), Chiara (S04) — independently give first-person testimony about work and participation, making this proposition's evidence base unusually well cross-referenced (§9), even though each source documents a different specific thing.

**Proposition 6** — the interesting phenomena emerge in the encounter between people, environments, and technologies, not in any one alone.
- NARRATIVE: the signal/frequency visual language (SpaceX poster, galaxy mural) that Jascha explicitly connects to his own relationship with sound ("sound never came easily to me") — a genuinely new, personal articulation of this proposition through visual/spatial metaphor, not argument.
- ARCHIVE: the RAMP beer collaboration with a Deaf brewer; aperitivo al buio's inversion of sighted/non-sighted roles.
- INTERPRETATION: the GAZE+TRANSLATION→SPACE/STRUCTURE hypothesis (§5) is itself essentially this proposition applied at the scale of the whole institution's origin.

**Proposition 7** — accessibility is a way of designing/observing/learning, not an endpoint.
- NARRATIVE: Nunzia's self-taught learning process ("observed, imitated, made mistakes, and learned"); the improvised, still-unfinished-at-opening construction process (§12).
- ARCHIVE: the multi-year maturation of the LIS/Deaf-programming thread (C-FB-25); the evolving "al buio" format naming (C-FB-23).
- INTERVIEW: interviewer commentary (S07-S10) on learning-through-encounter.
