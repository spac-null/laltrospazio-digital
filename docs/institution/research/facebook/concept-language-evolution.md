# Concept and Language Evolution, 2014–2025

**Working date:** 14 August 2026
**Source:** Facebook archive 2014–2025. See `facebook-archive-inventory.md` for methodology.

This tracks how the organisation's *own public voice* talks about disability, accessibility, inclusion, work, and itself — not word counts, but how words are used and what work they are doing in a sentence. Where the account shares *other people's* content (press articles, third-party posts), that is noted as a different register from the account's own original copy.

---

## 1. The overall arc, in one paragraph

The account moves from **no explicit disability vocabulary at all** (2014, where the deafblind framing comes entirely from an external Swiss press article, not the venue's own words) → **concrete, design-specific language tied to a single physical space** (2015: "a misura delle persone con disabilità," a venue built to fit disabled people) → **an "inclusione" umbrella term covering both disability and cultural/migrant difference, mixed with celebratory inspirational-disability sharing of third-party stories** (2016-17) → **explicit rights-based framing, especially around employment** (2018: "a fundamental right and not a privilege") → **personal, non-institutional testimony merging identity and practice** (2020: LIS as a founder's own daughter's first language) → **an activist/rights register borrowed from wider disability-justice movements, and a new vocabulary item ("neurodivergenti")** (2023) → **near-universal blanket branding hashtags (#Inclusione #Accessibilità) attached to almost every post regardless of relevance, even as the substantive practice being described in the same period becomes more sophisticated, not less** (2024-25). The marketing layer and the practice layer are not moving in lockstep by the end of the archive — see §7.

## 2. Disabilità / handicap

- **2014:** not used by the account itself. The only disability-adjacent term in this year comes from an external source it shares — "sordo-ciechi" (deafblind), used by Swiss public broadcaster TV Svizzera.
- **2015:** "persone con disabilità" is used for the first time in the account's own copy, in the opening-announcement post ("il nuovo locale ideato e costruito a misura delle persone con disabilità"). Also this year, a radio quote attributed to Nunzia Vannuccini: "Le persone con disabilità non sono fatte di cristallo: sono, appunto, persone" — an explicit pushback against fragility/pity framing.
- **2016-17:** "disabilità" appears mostly in **shared, third-party human-interest content** — a blind sculptor, an armless painter, a Deaf pianist — framed celebratory/inspirational ("dimostrazione del contrario," "non gli ha impedito di diventare un artista"). This "inspirational disability story" mode sits in real tension with the participatory-design ethos found elsewhere in the same years (see §7, Contradiction).
- **2018-19:** "normalità della disabilità" (3 May 2018, sharing a documentary) — an explicit normalising, anti-charity framing choice in the account's selection of what to amplify, even where the words are a third party's.
- **"Handicap"** is **never used by the account itself** anywhere in the archive read. (Working Paper v0.2's interview corpus does use "handicap" descriptively, via Maurice's testimony — this is a genuine register difference between the private/interview voice and the public Facebook voice, not a contradiction: the account appears to have avoided the word in its own public copy from at least 2018 onward, and no earlier use was found either.)
- **2023:** "**neurodivergenti**" appears for the first time (25 Apr) in a concrete accessibility-policy post (priority booking) — broadening the account's own working vocabulary beyond the physical/sensory categories used through 2014-2019 (deafblind, disabilità generically, non vedente, sordo).

## 3. Accessibilità

- Not used in the account's own 2014-15 copy (accessibility language in this window is external — the Dec 2015 SuperAbile magazine feature title, "Quando l'accessibilità è di casa," is a journalist's phrase, not the venue's).
- **2016-17:** "accessibilità" becomes tied to **concrete, describable actions** rather than slogans — e.g. 14 Jul 2016: "sottotitolato per le persone sorde e audiodescritto per le persone cieche" is a precise operational description of what was actually done, not a hashtag. Braille-captioned exhibitions (2017) extend the same operational precision to visual art.
- **2018:** Lonely Planet's third-party description (Braille menus, lowered counter, sign-language ordering) shows outside observers independently perceiving and naming the same concrete features — the word "accessibilità" is earned by physical facts a stranger can verify, not only asserted.
- **2022-25:** "accessibilità" increasingly functions as a **default hashtag** attached to nearly every post type — DJ nights, karaoke, food nights — regardless of whether that specific event has an accessibility feature distinct from the venue's general baseline. This is a real shift from the 2016-18 pattern of attaching the word specifically to posts describing a concrete accommodation.

## 4. Inclusione

- First self-applied use: **27 Oct 2015**, in the opening countdown ("Bologna avrà un luogo d'inclusione").
- **2016-17:** becomes the account's default umbrella self-description, attached as a hashtag to disability-related **and** migrant/cultural-difference content alike — i.e. "inclusione" is never disability-specific in this account's usage; it always covers both registers simultaneously from its first appearances.
- **2018:** rights-based inflection strengthens — the English hiring-policy post explicitly frames inclusion as "not… abstract concepts" but something "realized every day," directly linking the word to a concrete employment policy.
- **2023-25:** "inclusione"/"#Inclusione" becomes close to universal across all post types (world-food nights, DJ sets, poetry, markets) — the blanket-hashtag pattern noted in §3 for "accessibilità" applies equally here.

## 5. LIS / lingua dei segni / sordità

- **2014-15:** absent from the account's own copy (the earliest LIS-adjacent link, the Cineporto documentary, uses "sordo" in the film's own English/Italian framing, not the account's original text; the account's own framing in 2014 was "sordo-ciechi," via the external press).
- **2016:** the account describes Jascha Blume as *"filmmaker olandese sordo"* (Deaf) for the first time in an institutional post (RAMP beer launch).
- **2017:** the account uses *"non udente"* (non-hearing) for Jascha Blume instead, in his SetUp art-fair bio — **both terms ("sordo" and "non udente") co-exist in the account's own usage across 2016-17**, with no evidenced shift from one to the other; they appear to be used interchangeably by the same institution about the same person within a single year-pair.
- **2018-19:** LIS-specific in-house content is comparatively thin — most Deaf/LIS content in this window is third-party shared material (a sign-language classroom photo, a film share about a Deaf character) rather than the account's own programming description.
- **2020:** the register shifts to the deeply personal — Nunzia Vannuccini's own words state LIS "è la prima lingua di mia figlia" (is my daughter's first language) — a first-person, familial claim on the language rather than an institutional/programmatic one.
- **2021:** an externally-sourced quote (Redattore Sociale) — children asking for water "in Lis" — uses LIS as a marker of successful, unselfconscious normalisation, not a marked "accommodation."
- **2023:** the account's own copy reaches its most formally precise register yet, naming a specific university department, professor, and national institution (Ente Nazionale Sordi) in direct connection with a debate specifically about **teaching** LIS — moving from LIS as lived/ambient practice toward LIS as a subject of institutional/educational policy.
- **2025:** LIS/Deaf-hearing collaboration appears in an internationally-funded artistic register ("Sensory Dialogues," "Sub_Bar Show") — the language and the sensory/artistic exploration of Deaf experience are treated as **compositional material for art**, not an accommodation added afterward. This is the clearest archive-wide illustration of Working Paper v0.2's Proposition 2 becoming more, not less, sophisticated over time.

## 6. Lavoro / inserimento lavorativo / autonomia / partecipazione

- **2016 (1 Maggio):** the account's clearest early systemic statement — "l'inclusione inizia proprio dal diritto al lavoro" (inclusion begins precisely with the right to work) — explicitly frames inclusion as a labour-rights issue rather than hospitality or charity.
- **2018 (17 Aug, in English):** the fullest employment-policy statement in the archive, explicitly distinguishing legal-quota compliance ("Italian law 69/99") from stated organisational choice ("we make our choice to hire people with a disability first, and not as a last resort"), and using rights language ("a fundamental right and not a privilege").
- **2022:** a specific, self-reported workforce statistic appears for the first time — "80% dello staff" are workers with disabilities (Oct 2022) — the account's most quantified employment claim, though self-reported and uncorroborated.
- **"Autonomia" and "partecipazione"** are not used as frequently or as explicitly as "lavoro"/"inserimento lavorativo" in the account's own copy across the years read; where present, they appear mostly in shared third-party content (e.g. a 2016 Down-syndrome-autonomy-themed film share) rather than the account's own original framing. This is a genuine gap relative to Working Paper v0.2's own emphasis on autonomy and participation as core propositions — the FB record shows the underlying *practice* (employment, priority booking, co-design) more clearly than it shows those two specific *words*.

## 7. The central tension: activist/rights register vs. blanket-hashtag branding

By 2022-2023 two distinct registers are visible **simultaneously** in the account's own voice:

1. A **deepening, increasingly formal disability-rights/activist register** — Disability Pride Bologna participation (2023, "#disabilityjustice #disabilityrights"), the Comune "Diversity Team" collaboration (2023), the university-LIS-professor/Ente Nazionale Sordi event (2023), EU-funded Deaf/hearing art (2025).
2. A **genericised, near-universal branding layer** — "#Inclusione #Accessibilità" attached to karaoke nights, DJ sets, vintage markets, and pasta dinners regardless of any specific accessibility content in that particular post, becoming close to a default sign-off by 2024-25.

These two registers do not contradict each other, but they do pull in different directions: the first is substantively deepening (see `programme-history.md` §4 on the LIS thread's growing sophistication); the second risks the specific, concrete 2016-vintage precision ("sottotitolato per le persone sorde e audiodescritto per le persone cieche") diluting into generic marketing language that could describe almost any inclusive-minded venue. A future institutional voice (e.g. the website) should probably deliberately preserve the first register's specificity rather than defaulting to the second's genericism — this is a direct, practical implication for the "evidence architecture" and "avoid generic NGO aesthetics" guidance already present in Working Paper v0.2 §16.9.

## 8. Self-description of the organisation itself

- 2015: "il nuovo locale ideato e costruito a misura delle persone con disabilità" (design-specific).
- 2017: "il primo bar inclusivo d'Italia" (the first inclusive bar in Italy) — an unhedged, unsourced national-first claim (FB-C, treat as self-description only, not verified fact).
- 2018-19: "il locale culturale e accessibile," "un bar inclusivo" (via Lonely Planet's paraphrase).
- Recurring slogan across 2016-17: **"Ciò che sembrava impossibile, oggi è diventato possibile"** ("what seemed impossible has become possible") — used at least three times in different contexts (a beer launch, an anniversary, a follower-count milestone), functioning as an informal institutional motto specific to that period; not seen recurring in later years' posts.
- 2022: the venue-lineage recap post ("il primo l'Altro Spazio aperto nel 2015… e all'Altro Spazio al Pratello nato nel 2018") is the account's own clearest historical self-narration, useful as a primary source for the institutional timeline.
- 2024: "Coop L'Altro Spazio" — the only instance of the account naming its own legal/cooperative identity in its own public voice, in a single project-credit line (see `facebook-historical-evidence-register.md`).

## 9. Interpretive hypothesis: practice may run ahead of (or behind) its own language — status: INTERPRETATION TO TEST

**Owner-correction note:** the 2016-17 vocabulary documented above (§1-2, especially the "inspirational disability story" curatorial framing) is preserved deliberately and should **not** be rewritten into current terminology in any future revision. The uncomfortable-by-current-standards findings are part of the evidence, not an error to be smoothed over.

Read together, §1-8 above suggest a hypothesis worth testing rather than treating as proven:

> **Practice may sometimes have developed faster than the language used to describe it; later, institutional/accessibility vocabulary may sometimes have become broader than what any single activity alone could demonstrate.**

Two illustrations already in this document support taking the hypothesis seriously without over-claiming it:
- The 2016-17 "inspirational disability story" framing (§2) coexists with concrete, operationally precise practice in the same years (subtitling, audiodescription, a Deaf brewer as creative partner) — the *practice* in 2016-17 arguably outran the *language* used to narrate it, which still leaned on older charity/inspiration conventions.
- The 2024-25 blanket-hashtag pattern (§7) shows the reverse: by this point the *language* ("#Inclusione #Accessibilità" attached to nearly everything) may be claiming more, more generically, than any one specific post demonstrates on its own — even while the underlying practice (Sub_Bar Show, Sensory Dialogues) is simultaneously reaching its most sophisticated point in the archive.

This is offered as an **interpretation to test**, not a proven universal conclusion about the organisation's history — it may hold for some threads (LIS/Deaf programming) more than others (employment policy language, which is comparatively consistent and precise across 2016-2022), and a fuller test would need the interview corpus and current practice observation alongside this archive.
